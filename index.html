Step 02 · Operations
Step 02 · Operations

Build your team & your operations.

The work is finally tracked — jobs, costs, training, SOPs and reviews in one place — turning a group of workers into a team that keeps getting better.

This is how it works — watch the Operations demo
atrovia.co · Operations demo
What makes it different
  • Everything about how the work gets done, in one place — each part feeds the next. Job costing shows what a job took; the SOP library says how it’s done; training assigns SOPs by seat; reviews score against the real duties. The evidence for the next promotion already exists.
  • Your slowest step isn’t your problem. The constraint is computed, not configured — the stage running furthest past its own target, not the one that takes longest. Most owners reorganise around the wrong stage.
  • The free fix is always ranked before the paid one. Authority → training → cross-cover → process → a hire. It never buys capacity before the free moves are exhausted — and a hire needs two yeses.
  • Job costing where nobody types the hours. Labour comes from stage timestamps already recorded; only materials need entry, on sampled jobs. The variance corrects the rate — fixing every future quote of that service.
  • Capacity planned at your utilisation target, not flat out. And unsold capacity is the same number finance reads as its first growth lever — one number, one owner, two departments watching it.

It tells you what breaks if sales double — before sales double. And when the three worst stages are all one-person-deep, that’s a staffing shape, not three separate process problems. Naming the pattern is the product.

Step 05 · Finance

Connect QuickBooks. See it all.

The missing link — it connects sales, operations and the rest of the company into one set of numbers: the CFO a business your size can’t otherwise afford.

This is how it works — watch the Finance demo
atrovia.co · Finance demo
What makes it different
  • This is what ties the whole company together. Every department spends against a budget; the revenue forecast and sales target are one number from one source; the budget funds every seat the plan adds. See who owes you and for how long, what you owe and when, and a 13-week cash forecast that flags the tight week before you reach it.
  • Revenue-per-person is the balance number — and it names who owns the fix. Every other figure reports what already happened; this tells you whether the shape of the business is right, with a floor and target from your own books, and ranks the fixes by cost.
  • Markup is not margin — where most owners lose money. A 40% margin needs a 1.67× multiplier; most use 1.40× and get 28.6%. Atrovia builds the burdened cost of an hour, what a job consumes, and the price that holds a margin at your real overhead.
  • A dashboard tells you margin fell. This tells you it was never there. It names the step where it leaks and reprices it — the consultant’s one-time job, running every week.
  • Works on day one with nothing connected. Type the numbers and everything computes. Connect your books and it reads them nightly, upgrading every figure from estimate to measured. No integration is load-bearing.

The most common way a good small company dies is being busy, well-run, growing, and quietly losing money — because every decision that got it there looked reasonable at the time. This is the part that catches it.

What makes it different
