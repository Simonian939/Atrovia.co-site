Add-on & Marketplace Terms

Effective 13 September 2026 · Version 2026-09-13

The App Store offers two different things, and the difference matters. Some add-ons are built by Atrovia. Others connect you to a third-party service that bills you separately and sets its own rules. This page explains which is which, and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

The short version

Atrovia add-ons — Operations, Finance, Studio and anything else marked as ours. Covered by your Agreement, billed by us, supported by us.

Third-party add-ons — packs that include an outside provider, such as the Real Estate Pack with BatchData and iSpeedToLead. You hold the account with that provider, they bill you directly, their terms govern their service, and refunds for their data or leads come from them under their policies.

1. The two kinds of add-on

Every add-on in the App Store shows what it includes. Where an outside provider is involved, it is named on the card before you buy, along with the fact that it bills separately. We would rather lose a sale than have you discover a second invoice afterwards.

2. Atrovia add-ons

Built and run by us. Your End User License Agreement, Privacy Policy and AI Disclosure apply exactly as they do to the rest of the platform. Billed on your Atrovia invoice, cancellable at any time, effective at the next renewal, per the Credits & Billing Terms.

3. Third-party add-ons

A third-party add-on wires an outside service into Atrovia so you can work in one place. What it does not do is make us the provider of that service.

  • You contract directly with the provider. Their terms of service and privacy policy govern their part, and you should read them before enabling the add-on.
  • You hold the account and the credentials. Atrovia connects to it on your instruction and within the scope you grant.
  • We don't control their service. Availability, accuracy, pricing, support and any change they make are theirs. If they change an API or retire a product, the add-on may stop working — we'll tell you as soon as we know.
  • We are not liable for their acts or omissions, or for any loss arising from their data, their leads or their service.

The Atrovia fee shown for a third-party add-on, where there is one, is for the integration — the connection, the interface and the work of bringing that provider's data into your pipeline. It is not payment for the provider's data or leads.

We don't see, control or take a share of what you spend with them unless the App Store card says otherwise for that specific add-on.

5. Data quality, leads and refunds

Refunds for inaccurate data or bad leads are handled by the provider, under their own policies. Not by Atrovia. If a skip trace returns a wrong number or a purchased lead turns out to be junk, that claim goes to the company that sold it to you, and their refund policy decides the outcome.

We'll help you work out who to contact and what to send them. We can't overrule their decision.

No provider guarantees that a record is current or correct. People move, disconnect numbers and change names. Treat purchased data as a starting point to verify, not a fact. That is true of the best providers in the industry, and any vendor claiming otherwise is selling you something they can't deliver.

6. What purchased data may not be used for

The FCRA line

Property records, owner records and skip-traced contact data are marketing data. They are not consumer reports, and the providers are not consumer reporting agencies.

You may not use this data — or anything Atrovia produces from it — to decide whether to grant someone credit, employment, insurance, housing or tenancy, or for any other purpose covered by the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

This is not a formality. Using marketing data for a screening decision is an FCRA violation regardless of what the data cost or how accurate it turned out to be.

Contact data obtained through an add-on is also not consent to be texted or called. Everything in the Acceptable Use Policy still applies — consent, do-not-call scrubbing, opt-out, and the 8am–9pm window. A number you bought is a number, not a permission.

7. Data sharing when you enable an add-on

Enabling a third-party add-on authorises us to exchange data with that provider as the feature requires — for example, sending a property address to get back an owner record, or receiving a lead into your pipeline.

  • We send only what the feature needs, never your whole account.
  • The provider's privacy policy governs what they do with it once it reaches them.
  • You confirm you have the right to send them whatever you send.
  • Data that comes back into Atrovia is covered by our Privacy Policy like any other data in your account.

8. Turning an add-on off

  • Turn off any add-on in the App Store. The Atrovia fee stops at your next renewal; there's no partial refund for the current period.
  • Turning off the add-on does not close your account with the provider, and does not stop their charges. Cancel with them directly — this catches people out, so we'll remind you on screen when you disable it.
  • Data already in your Atrovia account stays there. Leads you bought are yours.

9. Availability, previews and "Soon"

  • An add-on marked Soon is not available, is not being sold, and carries no promised date.
  • Add-ons offered as a preview or beta are provided as-is, may change or be withdrawn, and are excluded from any service commitment — see section 11 of the Agreement.
  • We may add, change, reprice or retire an add-on. If we retire one you're paying for, we'll give you notice and a pro-rata refund of the unused Atrovia portion.

Everything else in your End User License Agreement applies to add-ons, including the disclaimers and the limitation of liability in sections 16 and 17.
